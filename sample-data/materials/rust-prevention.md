# Preventing Rust and Corrosion

Rust is iron oxide formed when iron reacts with oxygen and moisture. Galvanic corrosion accelerates when dissimilar metals touch in the presence of an electrolyte, so designers isolate incompatible metals or use a protective barrier. Stainless steel resists corrosion through a chromium-rich passive film but can still suffer pitting in harsh environments.

Coatings, drainage, and regular inspection are often more effective than choosing a single perfect material. Sacrificial zinc can protect attached steel by corroding first. Paint and powder coatings delay water contact, while careful design prevents crevices where wet conditions persist.
