# Tide-Pool Adaptations

Tide pools are small marine habitats repeatedly exposed to air, heat, salinity changes, and waves. Limpets use strong suction to resist waves and migrate away from drying areas. Mussels close their shells to limit water loss, while anemones retract tentacles when disturbed. Seaweeds tolerate brief dryness but grow rapidly when submerged.

These overlapping adaptations let many species share a narrow coastal zone. Organisms that maintain moisture, avoid predators, or anchor securely can survive the changing conditions between high tides.
