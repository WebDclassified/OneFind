# Jazz Turnarounds and ii–V–I

A turnaround connects one harmonic section to the next, usually by moving through a ii–V–I progression. In C major, the final cadence might use D minor, G major, and C major. These chords create a smooth, forward pull while preserving voice-leading.

Improvising players also use substitutions, such as tritone substitution, to vary the tension. A turnaround can be rhythmically simple or syncopated. The important skill is hearing the harmonic route ahead and choosing substitutions that remain connected to the melody rather than treating chord names as isolated labels.
