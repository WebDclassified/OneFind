# Crop Rotation and Soil Fertility

Crop rotation grows different plant families in sequence across a field and across seasons. Legumes can contribute nitrogen through symbiotic bacteria in their root nodules. Deep-rooted crops pull water and nutrients from lower soil layers, while cover crops protect bare ground and feed soil organisms between cash crops.

A useful rotation interrupts pest life cycles, reduces repeated nutrient demand, and makes residues available at different times. Legumes, cereals, roots, and cover crops have different effects, so a three-year sequence is often more resilient than repeatedly planting the same crop.
