# Captioning for Accessible Audio and Video

Accessible captions represent speech, important sounds, speaker identity, and relevant non-speech information. Automatic speech recognition can create a useful first draft, but names, technical terms, numbers, and overlapping speakers usually need human review.

Captions should be synchronized, readable, and positioned consistently. A caption file may also identify non-speech events such as [applause], [door opens], or [music begins]. Color indicators, speaker labels, and descriptive transcripts make the same content easier to follow for people with different hearing, language, and attention needs.
