# Bicycle Gearing and Gear-Inches

A bicycle gear combines the front chainring with a rear cassette. Gear-inches describe the approximate distance traveled for one pedal revolution: chainring teeth multiplied by wheel diameter, divided by cassette teeth. A lower gear-inches value provides easier torque for hills, while a higher value covers more ground per pedal stroke.

Derailleurs guide the chain between sprockets. Riders balance gearing with cadence, terrain, and tire pressure. Cross-chaining between very small and very large sprockets is avoided because it increases wear and can reduce shifting precision.
