# Geometry of a Geodesic Dome

A geodesic dome approximates a sphere with many triangular facets. A classic geodesic construction divides an icosahedron’s triangular faces into smaller triangles, projecting the resulting vertices onto a sphere. The circumradius is the distance from the center to any surface vertex.

Because the load spreads through a triangulated network, domes can be very efficient relative to their weight. However, a true sphere cannot be tiled by identical flat triangles, so practical panels differ in size and angle. Openings and foundations must be designed carefully without disrupting the shell’s structural continuity.
