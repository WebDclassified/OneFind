# Composting and Nutrient Balance

Composting turns organic material into stable humus through decomposition. A balanced mixture includes carbon-rich dry material such as leaves and nitrogen-rich material such as food scraps. A carbon-to-nitrogen ratio near 30 parts carbon to 1 part nitrogen gives microbes enough energy without creating odors.

Aeration introduces oxygen, while moisture should resemble a wrung-out sponge. Windrows can be turned to control heat and speed decomposition. Methanogens thrive in oxygen-poor conditions, so compact or waterlogged piles may smell. Finished compost is dark, crumbly, and contains nutrients for plant roots.
