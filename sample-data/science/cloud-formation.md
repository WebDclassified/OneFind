# How Clouds Form

Clouds begin when air rises, expands, and cools adiabatically. Cooling brings the air closer to saturation, and condensation nuclei such as dust, salt, or smoke provide surfaces on which water droplets form. The lifted air is called the conditionally unstable layer, while the warmer environment around it creates the cap that limits growth.

Cumulus clouds form in rising columns and often develop into showers. Layered clouds appear when broad lifting turns moisture into thin sheets. Cloud height, droplet size, and the surrounding temperature profile all affect whether precipitation reaches the surface.
